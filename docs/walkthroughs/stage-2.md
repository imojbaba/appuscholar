# Stage 2 walk-through: Idea and Literature

Definition of done for stage 2 (Idea & Feasibility, Research, Research helpers, Analyse a paper, the Reading shelf and the Citation Manager): a student completes this journey on the live site. Tests passing is necessary, not sufficient.
The same twelve steps run automatically in `apps/web/e2e/stage2-idea-literature.spec.ts`, with the AI answered from recorded replies and OpenAlex, PubMed and Crossref answered from recorded responses; its steps carry the numbers of the rows below, which are the twelve steps of the v1 chapter's "Student journey through Idea and Literature". Keep the two in step. The e2e also reads every stage blob back from the api after the journey and checks it is ciphertext; on the live site the database itself is the check (step 12's note). Fix wave A changed the behaviour behind rows 2 to 11; the expectations below are the new ones, and `docs/walkthroughs/fix-wave-a.md` is the separate sheet that walks each fix on its own (the e2e's steps 11b, 13 and 14 belong to it).

**Site:** https://appuscholar-web.vercel.app
**You need:**
- **A throwaway account**: a new email address you can sign in with, so the terms and the welcome window appear. Use it for nothing else.
- **Your Groq or OpenAI key** for the AI steps (2, 3, 5, 7, 8, 10). A passphrase of at least 10 characters that you will remember: step 1 sets up the vault with it, it locks the key and every stage's data, and the walk types it several times. Keep the recovery sheet step 1 shows.
- **The real OpenAlex, PubMed and Crossref.** Your browser calls them directly (the app's server never sees these requests). They receive only what v1 sends: search words (step 3: your topic, the related terms you keep and the kind-of-study words), the PubMed ids (PMIDs) of the hits PubMed found, to fetch their summaries and abstracts (step 3), OpenAlex ids (step 5: connected papers), DOIs (steps 6 and 11: the retraction check; step 8 if the paper's reference list prints DOIs; step 11: Crossref), and, for a source without a DOI, its title and the first author's surname (step 11: Crossref). Step 4 opens Google Scholar in a new tab with your topic typed in. No AI key and no text of yours goes to these services.
- **An open-access PDF** for step 7: you download it in step 5 or 6 with a result's "📄 Free full text" button (a result with the green "Free · gold" badge), or any PDF of a paper you are allowed to read. A PDF with no readable text (a scan without a text layer) is refused on purpose; `apps/web/e2e/fixtures/sample-paper.pdf` is a PDF with almost no readable text (under 200 characters), if you want to see the refusal.
- **A desktop browser** at about 1440 px wide, with its bookmarks bar shown (step 4 drags a link onto it), and allowed to open pop-up windows for this site (step 11).
- A pen for the recovery code.

**Record:** fill the last column for each step with `VERIFIED` or `FAILED` and the date (`YYYY-MM-DD`). Anything FAILED is fixed and re-run before stage 2 is called done. Build under test: read the "Version ..." pill in the header (top left) and write it here: `________`. It reads "Version " and the first seven characters of the commit that was built, so it must match the commit you meant to test; if it does not, you are looking at an older build.

## What differs from v1 on purpose

Each line points at its row in `docs/superpowers/specs/parity-deviations.md`; the row has the detail and the reason.

- D-S2-1: Research's sentences that v1 has only in English stay English in Hindi too, until Dr. Sharma gives the Hindi.
- D-S2-2: the same for Analyse a paper.
- D-S2-3: the same for the Reading shelf.
- D-S2-4: your OpenAlex key, when you save one, is added to OpenAlex requests as in v1, but it is kept with your AI keys, not in this browser.
- D-S2-5: PubMed's abstracts are read by the app's own small reader instead of the browser's; the abstracts are the same.
- D-S2-6: when OpenAlex's free daily budget runs out the sentence is v1's; only the way the app passes it along differs.
- D-S2-7: your idea's four boxes belong to the project and are locked in your vault (v1 kept one set for the whole browser); a failed assessment also shows a toast.
- D-S2-8: the Research helpers read a selection of several paragraphs with line breaks between them (v1 ran the last word of one into the next); a failed helper also shows a toast.
- D-S2-9: the citation library belongs to the project and is locked in your vault; buttons that need a document or a project wait for it instead of failing.
- D-S2-10: the library's checks keep their results per project, in the vault, and the "cited in your documents" counts follow your typing.
- D-S2-11: the capture, the reference list, the classic sources and the library import write into the project's library in the vault; a capture asks before the passphrase, and is lost if you are signed out when you click the bookmark.
- D-S2-12: search results stay in the tab and are never stored; the "Rank by fit" box is remembered per project; the OpenAlex key is locked like an AI key.
- D-S2-13: a paper hidden by a filter stays ticked and comes back ticked (v1 could tick the wrong paper after filtering).
- D-S2-14: the Reading shelf belongs to the project and is kept in your vault on the server (v1 kept it in this browser only); still only the text, never the PDF.
- D-S2-15: lectures and the lesson say "arrive in a later phase"; the shelf list in Analyse a paper reads the vault.
- D-S2-16: paper cards and the chosen columns belong to the project and are kept in your vault.
- D-S2-17: the pop-out is a page of its own (`/popout?tool=cite`); a reference list sent from it reaches the writing window (in v1 it was lost).
- D-S2-18: sentences v1 builds in its script stay English in Hindi too.
